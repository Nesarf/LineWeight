# The books, and how much of each could be read

**Why the distinction is recorded.** A book whose text extracts cleanly and a book that can only be learned by looking
at its pages are different kinds of evidence. **The first can be quoted; the second can only be looked at**, and a page
kept from it is the only way it can be consulted again. Marking the difference stops a page of pictures being cited as
though it were a paragraph.

All fifteen live in `E:\DaShaoHuo\downloads\books\`, downloaded from archive.org. **The PDFs are not in this repository**
— they are somebody else's books, and what belongs here is what was learned from them, not the books themselves.

**And the arrangement of the whole shelf, once read:**

- **Hampton** names the three stages and keeps saying what not to do.
- **Bradley** is the only one that gives folds as mechanics rather than as decoration.
- **comic-inking** is the only one that treats line width as information.
- **Hayashi** has the best teaching device — the same figure drawn four ways.
- **Famous Artists** has the best map — a course whose contents page is the syllabus of everything above.
- **Tezuka** has the only chapter built on combination rather than on description.
